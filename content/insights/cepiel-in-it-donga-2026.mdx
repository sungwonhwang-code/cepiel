On 30 October, *IT Dong-A* ran a profile of CEPiEL as part of its series on
the **Start-up NEST 19** cohort — the scale-up programme run by the Korea
Credit Guarantee Fund (KODIT) with The Invention Lab as accelerator. The
original article is in Korean; this is not a translation, but my own short
summary of what we talked about, for readers outside Korea.

## The problem: data is not the bottleneck any more

Most chemical plants already have thousands of sensors. Collecting data is
the easy part. The hard part is turning that data into a change in how the
plant is actually run — and doing it in a way the people responsible for the
plant will trust.

When I moved into academia after 13 years in industry (GS E&amp;C, AspenTech,
UOP), the
same frustration followed me. Plant software is still mostly imported, and it
is fragmented: process simulation, data management, predictive maintenance,
and advanced process control each sit in their own tool, under their own
licence, and rarely talk to each other. As I put it in the interview:

> "Every time you want to add a capability, you buy another licence — and the
> pieces still don't connect." *(translated from Korean)*

CEPiEL started from a simpler observation: the data-reconciliation and
AI-based optimisation methods we developed at [CEPI Lab](https://cepi.inha.ac.kr)
were already delivering results in real plants. The goal is to bring them
together on one platform, at a cost a mid-sized manufacturer can justify.

## What we're building

Two products, one pipeline:

- **CEPiDTS™** — the digital-twin platform. Sensor data is cleaned and
  reconciled, checked against a first-principles process model in near
  real time, and then used to train ML models that recommend operating
  conditions.
- **CEPiML™** — the AI training and optimisation engine underneath. It
  auto-selects and tunes from 16+ ML algorithms, including
  physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that put the governing equations
  directly into training.

A few design choices are worth calling out.

**Soft sensors built from CFD.** Some of the most important variables in a
plant — temperature profiles inside a reactor, compositions you can't sample
— are expensive or impossible to measure. By training on CFD results, we can
estimate them at close to real-time speed, instead of waiting days for a
full CFD run. We keep the nonlinear first-principles model and learn
alongside it, rather than replacing it.

**Open simulators, no lock-in.** Commercial simulators are excellent and also
expensive, which is why many mid-sized companies never start a digital-twin
project at all. CEPiDTS can run open-source simulators such as DWSIM and
OpenFOAM, and it connects to the commercial simulators a customer already
owns. You keep the models you have and adopt in stages.

**An AI that explains, not one that drives.** Operators can ask questions in
plain language, and the agent answers with analysis, plots, and — most
importantly — *why* a given operating point is optimal. Because the reasoning
sits on a physical model, an engineer can verify it. That matters even more
in regulated industries like pharmaceuticals and semiconductors.

> "The plant should find its optimum — but an engineer must be able to verify
> the reasoning." *(translated from Korean)*

A plant that stops for even a short time loses a lot of money. So we are
deliberately not building a system that takes over the controls. We are
building one that helps the people in the control room make a better
decision, and leaves that decision with them.

## Starting with utilities

Our first target is the part of the plant every industry shares: the
**utility system** — steam, cooling water, chillers. Utilities are a large
share of a site's energy use, have many interacting variables, and are still
largely run on operator experience. That makes them the right place to prove
value before moving into core production units.

Since CEPiML launched in April, the work has picked up: a digital-twin project
for an FPSO topside process as part of a consortium with a global process
software company, an anomaly-detection project on an EDC cracker for a Korean
chemical company, discussions on a utility digital twin with a petrochemical
producer, and an HVAC energy-optimisation project in Singapore. On an LNG
liquefaction pilot with a Korean shipbuilder, a CEPiDTS proof of concept cut
energy use by roughly **5–6%**.

## What's next

Next is **CEPiML 2.0**: a chemical-process ontology — a machine-readable map
of how equipment, variables, and operating documents relate — combined with
an LLM interface, so an engineer can talk to the whole plant in natural
language. Alongside that, with our co-CEO Paul Cho and the KhaiEL
team, we are working on Singapore as a first hub abroad and on the North
American market.

> "I want to show that a process-AI company born in Korea can work alongside
> global commercial software and still give customers a more reasonable
> choice." *(translated from Korean)*
